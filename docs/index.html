Session findings · 2026-10-05

Four results that changed the build

1. Our own holdout was misleading

Re-scoring all 31 previously submitted rasters on the four-fold catalogue-ablation holdout gives a Spearman correlation of −0.10 with their real public scores (−0.27 including two maps whose pruning leaked the held-out folds). Withheld mapped faults are not a stand-in for the expert-only hidden faults. Model selection was therefore moved onto the public-score calibration.

2. Dottedness dominates everything

Of five map-level descriptors, clumpiness is by far the strongest predictor of the public score (Spearman −0.751, leave-one-out R² 0.553 alone). The metric takes a maximum over a 300 m neighbourhood, so adjacent predictions buy almost no extra recall and pay full false-positive weight. Every top map is perfectly dotted; every bottom map is clumped.

3. Predicting near the catalogue is wasted budget

The controlled pair in the record: removing all mass within 2 px of a catalogued fault from a 44,090-pixel map left its kernel-weighted recall unchanged (0.392 → 0.394) while deleting 6,436 pixels, and the score rose 0.2600 → 0.2778. Those pixels contributed zero recall. This build uses a 600 m (6 px) stand-off and places 0.0% of its mass within 300 m of a catalogued fault.

4. Catalogue-geometry features actively hurt

On the ablation holdout, adding distance-to-catalogue, fault-density, fault-tip and along-strike-continuation features dropped pooled DTI from 0.167 to 0.053 — below the matched-budget random floor of 0.065. A model told where the map already is will retrace the map. This build's 234 features are strictly label-free.

Honest negative result: pure multi-scale persistence used on its own as a ranking scored 0.043 on the holdout, below the random floor. Persistence earns its place in this build as one feature family among 234 and as a stability certificate — not as a standalone fault detector.

How we work

Evidence before confidence

What this project will do

  • Use official challenge rules and disclose what data came from unauthenticated mirrors.
  • Preserve a fixed hypothesis, detector, and promotion gate before looking at raster outcomes.
  • Compare candidates against the public catalogue under spatial separation and an equal pixel budget.
  • Make the TIFF fail closed on any output-format or promotion-gate failure.

What it will not claim

  • That public catalogue associations reproduce expert fault labels or hidden scoring.
  • That persistent topographic/geophysical edges prove mineralization.
  • That an unverified historical submission is an organizer-confirmed score.
  • That a good pseudo-holdout result guarantees a top leaderboard position.
Current provenance limitation: the competition-grid rasters used to build the downloadable TIFF were reconstructed here from a GitHub data bridge built from the user-supplied Dropbox mirrors, not from a fresh participant-authenticated DrivenData download in this sandbox.
