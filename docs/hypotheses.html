Current evidence class: the competition-grid rasters used here were reconstructed from a GitHub data bridge built from the user-provided Dropbox mirrors. They are hash-pinned and internally consistent, but they were not freshly downloaded from the authenticated DrivenData data tab in this sandbox.

Why the ranking changed

The original GEMSDOE37 plan centered on DEM/magnetic/gravity persistence alone. Once the competition-grid rasters were available, that H1 route was actually executed. It built successfully but produced only 25,686 positive cells—below the fixed 37,654-pixel budget—so it could not directly support a same-budget holdout comparison. Quick exact-mask screens on the actual bands then showed that the geodetic strain fields were stronger than the original DEM/magnetic/gravity control on this proxy. That moved strain to the top of the ranking and demoted pure persistence to a prior rather than the whole detector.

What changed scientifically

Topology became a prior, not the whole detector

The H1 persistence surface did not outperform by itself under the exact-budget gate. The published candidate therefore keeps topology as a stability prior but lets the strain fields drive the rank ordering. That is more faithful to the measured holdout evidence in this repository.

What did not change

Persistence is still not geology

Even inside H2, the persistence term remains a ranking prior. It does not prove line continuity, geological identity, or private-label value. The catalogue pseudo-holdout is still only a proxy.

Promotion rule actually used

The published TIFF was promoted only after the final H2 rank-mix beat its registered strain-only incumbent by more than +0.005 pooled DTI, improved at least 3 of 4 folds, and avoided any fold regression below −0.02. It passed all three conditions:

  • Pooled gain: +0.00884
  • Fold gains: NW +0.00480, NE +0.00264, SW +0.03363, SE +0.00327
  • Positive folds: 4/4
