H1 · preregistered technical plan

Persistence is a filter.
Not a fault certificate.

The detector rewards ridge/edge responses that persist in threshold and remain spatially trackable across smoothing scales. It does not establish geological identity; the only promotion evidence is a matched-budget, spatially blocked catalogue pseudo-holdout.

1 · Scalar fields & topology

From bands to persistence-weighted scores

The leading hypothesis uses metadata-identified detrended elevation, magnetic potential-field signal and isostatic gravity. The pipeline refuses to infer band order when tags are absent or ambiguous; it does not assume the mirror band order.

  1. Normalize the source bands. Fit robust 2nd–98th percentile input ranges; clip normalized values to [0,1]. In each spatial fold, these quantiles are fitted only on the other three complete quadrants. Full-grid feature values remain available as unlabeled prediction covariates.
  2. Build physical responses. DEM response is scale-normalized Hessian anisotropy, designed to emphasize a line where cross-ridge curvature exceeds along-ridge curvature (convex and concave forms). Magnetic and gravity responses are scale-normalized horizontal-gradient magnitudes. Each response is robustly mapped using its 50th and 99.5th percentiles, fit on the same training blocks.
  3. Use mask-aware smoothing. Gaussian smoothing at σ=1, 2, 4 pixels (100, 200, 400 m) divides the smoothed valid values by smoothed support, then rejects locations with less than 95% Gaussian-kernel support.
  4. Compute H0 superlevel persistence. For each nonnegative normalized response, consider the connected superlevel sets as the threshold descends. A local component is born at a peak and dies when it merges into an older component. Eight-neighbour connectivity is fixed. The finite-bar lifetime is birth minus death; the essential domain component is omitted.
  5. Track nearby bars between adjacent scales. Greedy one-to-one peak matching is ordered by persistence and must lie within 2 pixels. Retain a track seen at ≥2 consecutive scales, with per-scale lifetime ≥0.05 and a positive margin over 2ε; ε=0.01 in normalized response units. Require nonzero support from at least two of DEM curvature, magnetic edge and gravity edge families.
  6. Fuse and rank. Persistence-weighted layer responses are fused as a rank signal. The values are not calibrated probabilities and do not encode a posterior likelihood of fault presence.
Stability caveat: the Cohen–Steiner, Edelsbrunner & Harer theorem bounds persistence-diagram bottleneck distance by the L∞ perturbation between two fixed scalar functions. With ε=0.01, a bar longer than 2ε cannot be matched to the diagonal under that assumed perturbation bound. This project has not empirically established ε=0.01 for the raster noise. The theorem does not certify a geological ridge and does not cover the heuristic matching of bars between Gaussian scales.

Implementation receipts retain per-scale bar counts, observed track counts, minimum persistence and stability margins, matching rule, layer-family agreement, input hashes, and all fixed parameters. Every accepted track's per-scale birth, death, lifetime, peak row/column, and sigma is stored in a hash-pinned gzip JSONL sidecar under ignored `data/prepared/`; the holdout report links each sidecar. See H0 code, ridge/fusion code, and preregistration JSON.

2 · Competition metric

Credit near faults; penalize distant mass

The official problem page describes a distance-weighted Tversky objective with α=0.2, β=0.8 and 300 m support on the 100 m grid. The reference implementation is independently coded here and should be checked against the organizer’s published worked example before interpreting real fold scores.

DTI = TP_w / (TP_w + α·FP_w + β·FN_w + ε)

For each truth pixel, TP_w uses the strongest nearby prediction multiplied by a linearly decreasing distance kernel; FN_w is unmatched truth credit. Predicted mass outside fault support contributes to FP_w. The local code’s worked-example parity is a pending verification item; no score from it is presented as an organizer result. Official problem/metric page ↗

Why not publish the soft rank surface directly? To compare methods under a controlled prediction budget, validation turns each ranking into an exact top-k binary map. At submission time these selected cells are written as 1 and all other grid cells as 0, staying in [0,1].

3 · Spatial validation & publication

Four guarded catalogue folds

The truth is the public USGS/INGENIOUS catalogue raster. NW, NE, SW and SE are held out in turn; each fold's robust normalization is fitted on the other three complete quadrants. Evaluation masks omit a 300 m guard around quadrant boundaries and outer raster edges. The detector is unsupervised: catalogue values are not used for feature selection, calibration, or score construction.

The comparison uses the same positive-pixel budget as a pinned historical H33-2-B2 raster. It is an educational owner artifact with a SHA-256 from the owner's audit receipt; the score/file attribution is not verified by DrivenData. It is read only to define a baseline and budget—the output pixels are selected from GEMSDOE37's independently computed H1 score surface, never copied from the reference.

Promotion gate (fixed in advance)

  • H1 mean paired DTI improvement over historical reference is at least 0.005.
  • H1 improves at least 3 of 4 spatial folds and no fold regresses by more than 0.02.
  • H1 also beats the matched-budget single-scale control: positive mean improvement, at least 3 positive folds, and no fold regression worse than −0.02.
  • Only after both comparisons pass does the script generate a unique TIFF and update the site's submission-status JSON. Failed gates write no new TIFF.
Interpretation: this is spatially blocked recovery of public catalogue lines—not expert-only validation, proof of unlisted-fault discovery, hidden/private-label performance, or a guaranteed public/private leaderboard improvement. No holdout result exists in this checkout.
4 · Output contract

Fail closed on the actual saved TIFF

The output writer copies the official sample-template grid and reopens the file to check one band, float32 type, EPSG:32611, exact affine transform/dimensions/resolution/bounds, absent nodata tag, finite values for every cell, range [0,1] for every cell, exact matched positive-pixel budget, and SHA-256. Zero is written outside the candidate footprint to prevent sentinel leakage. Unit tests exercise valid output, out-of-range values, shape mismatches, and invalid sentinels.

The portal error “Predicted values must be in range [0, 1]” is addressed by checking stored cell values—not only the in-footprint region. A format pass cannot rescue a failed scientific gate.

5 · Known limits & next steps

What remains uncertain

  • Competition inputs are user-provided Dropbox mirrors, not authenticated DrivenData files. Their contents, tags, provenance, licensing and official identity must be reviewed locally.
  • Data download and all raster evaluation remain blocked. The true held-out local best, gate outcome, candidate TIFF, and organizer score are unknown.
  • The historical baseline's public score/file identity is unverified; DTI implementation parity against the organizer's worked example remains pending.
  • The 0.01 perturbation bound is a preregistered conditional assumption, not a measured sensor-error bound. Cross-scale persistence tracks are heuristic, not multiparameter persistence.
  • Catalogue pseudo-holdout labels omit expert-only additions by design and may favor known, accessible faults. Hidden-label behavior cannot be inferred from this proxy.
  • H2–H4 remain proposals; their external data coverage, exact licenses, and test outcomes are not established.
All performance claims require a recorded report, fold table, hashes, and source class.“Maximize P(Win)” · “Own the Outcome.”
