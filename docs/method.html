1 · Scalar fields & topology

From tagged bands to persistence-weighted scores

The preregistration selects metadata-identified detrended elevation, magnetic potential-field signal, and isostatic gravity. Band order is not guessed from file position. The current code is an H0 component-maxima detector, not a supervised fault classifier.

  1. Normalize source bands. Fit robust 2nd–98th percentile ranges and clip to [0,1]. In each spatial fold, fit on the other three complete quadrants; apply the fixed transforms across the unlabeled feature grid.
  2. Build scalar responses. DEM response is scale-normalized Hessian anisotropy; magnetic and gravity responses are scale-normalized horizontal-gradient magnitudes. Each response is robustly scaled using fitted 50th/99.5th percentiles.
  3. Smooth with nodata-aware support. Use Gaussian σ=1, 2, 4 pixels (100, 200, 400 m). Normalize by valid support and reject cells with less than 95% kernel support.
  4. Compute finite H0 superlevel bars. As the threshold descends, track connected components of `{x : response(x) ≥ threshold}` using 8-neighbour connectivity. Each finite bar records a component birth peak and merge/death threshold; lifetime is `birth − death`. Essential components are omitted.
  5. Apply the registered lifetime and tracking rules. Retain bars with persistence ≥0.05. The conditional sup-norm assumption is ε=0.01, so the minimum margin above 2ε is 0.03. Greedily match peak coordinates between adjacent scales within 2 pixels, require at least two observed scales, and require support from at least two independent layer families. The `epsilon` value is not measured sensor error.
  6. Score matched maxima. Persistence/layer weights are applied to the response around matched peaks; the current implementation paints a 5×5 peak neighborhood at most. It does not impose explicit elongation, orientation continuity, or a line-shaped connected-output constraint. Therefore its output is not itself a fault trace.
Stability caveat: for a fixed scalar function, the Cohen–Steiner, Edelsbrunner & Harer theorem bounds persistence-diagram bottleneck distance by the L∞ function perturbation. A bar longer than 2ε cannot match the diagonal if the perturbation is bounded by ε. Here ε=0.01 is a preregistered sensitivity assumption, not an empirically established raster-error bound. The theorem does not certify geology and does not cover heuristic matching across Gaussian smoothing scales.

The fused score is a rank signal, not a calibrated probability. Receipts are designed to retain per-scale bar counts, births/deaths/lifetimes, peak coordinates, sigma, track rule, input hashes, and fixed parameters when data are available. See H0 code, ridge/fusion code, and preregistration JSON.

2 · Competition metric

Credit near new truth; penalize unsupported mass

The official problem description specifies α=0.2, β=0.8, and triangular distance support to 300 m on the 100 m grid. At a conceptual level:

k(d) = max(1 − d / 300 m, 0)
DTI = TP_w / (TP_w + 0.2·FP_w + 0.8·FN_w)

For each truth pixel, `TP_w` uses the strongest nearby prediction times the distance kernel; `FN_w` is the remaining unmatched truth credit. Each prediction adds distance-weighted `FP_w` unless it receives nearby truth support. The local proxy adds 1e-8 for numerical safety; no independent parity test against organizer code has been run. Synthetic metric tests, including arithmetic for the published rounded example, pass locally as part of the 37-test suite (2026-10-05); this is not organizer-code parity. Official metric/format page ↗

Exact official known-fault mask: only the provided known-fault pixels are excluded. There is no proximity buffer. Nearby unmasked pixels are scored normally, and new/corrected/splayed truth may occur within 300 m of known traces. See the staff clarification and new-fault definition.

For matched-budget comparisons, each ranking selects an exact number of highest positive scores with deterministic row-major tie-breaking. The local test summary sums TP/FP/FN across disjoint blocks and computes one pooled DTI, matching the organizer's stated aggregation. The arithmetic mean of per-fold DTIs is descriptive only. 3 · Spatial validation & promotion

Four guarded catalogue pseudo-holdout folds

The only local truth source is still the public USGS/INGENIOUS catalogue raster. NW, NE, SW, and SE are withheld in turn; each fold's normalization is fit on the other three complete quadrants. A 300 m inner boundary guard is used to reduce cross-block metric-kernel leakage. That guard is a local validation design, not an organizer mask, and makes this holdout unable to test corrections/splays within 300 m of visible mapped traces.

The held-out catalogue is a proxy for transfer to other mapped faults, not expert-only hidden new-fault truth. In the exact-mask scorer, only visible-known pixels are excluded; the fold design therefore remains a limited proxy for near-trace new-fault scoring.

The matched positive-pixel budget is fixed at 37,654 from the owner-reported H33-2-B2 count. This count is a budget only; no H33 prediction pixels are reused. The H2 publication instead compares a new rank-mix against a newly measured strain-only incumbent on the same grid, folds, and exact mask.

3b · Instrument validity & the emission rule

What the local screens can and cannot decide

Two independent screens are used, and both are published with their measured failure to rank the live board. The catalogue-fold screen withholds quadrant blocks of the published catalogue (40 px guard, catalogue ablation inside the scored domain). The SGMC-prevalence screen subsamples whole connected components of the public USGS State Geologic Map Compilation structure population that lies more than 300 m from every catalogue pixel, to the truth mass implied by the score-ladder inversion.

Consequence: the screens are a falsification gate — a candidate must beat matched-budget random placement under an identical emission protocol and must not fall below chance on either screen — but they cannot certify, rank or forecast a live gain. The published geometry is therefore matched to the best publicly scored precedent (37,654 dots at a mean 29.0 px = 2.9 km from the catalogue), not to any local optimum. Full derivation and tables: research/instrument_validity.md.

Honest reading: the ladder regression has eight points, all from one owner, with dot budget and stand-off partly collinear. It is monotone evidence, not a causal law, and it gives the ranking field no credit at all.
4 · Output contract

Validate the stored TIFF against the template

The local writer requires every stored value to be finite float32 in `[0,1]` and omits a nodata tag; this is a conservative policy, not verified outside-footprint guidance. It also checks one band, exact dimensions, CRS, affine transform, resolution, bounds, matched positive-pixel budget, and SHA-256. Candidate cells outside model support are zero-valued.

Synthetic writer tests cover grid and range validation. No authenticated competition example has been tested in the portal, and outside-footprint encoding remains unverified. A format check cannot rescue a failed scientific gate.

5 · Limits & blockers

What remains uncertain

  • Competition inputs were reconstructed from a GitHub bridge of user-provided Dropbox mirrors, not freshly authenticated from the DrivenData data tab in this sandbox. Their organizer provenance remains unverified here.
  • The downloadable TIFF has no organizer receipt, portal-acceptance receipt, public-board row, or private-label evaluation. It is holdout-screened only.
  • H33's user-supplied 0.2778 association is still unverified. Its owner report gave a projection and an unscored proxy; the official B=2 mask explanation is incorrect under staff clarification.
  • ε=0.01 remains an assumption. H0 maxima and greedy cross-scale tracks are not geological lines or formal multiparameter persistence.
  • The local screens are anti-correlated with (or flat against) the public ladder, so no local number in these receipts is a forecast. The H2 rank-mix alternate remains published, but its pseudo-holdout gain (0.0281 → 0.0369) is not evidence of a leaderboard gain.
  • The public 1 m LiDAR scarp stack covers roughly a third of the footprint and its uint8 quantisation is lossy (q = 1 + round(254·t(x/xmax)), 0 = no LiDAR); roads, channels and terrace risers also create steps, per the product’s own caveats.
  • External sources actually used are now hash-pinned products: USGS GeoDAWN radiometric/extension rasters (DOI 10.5066/P93LGLVQ) and the USGS 3DEP-derived LiDAR scarp stack; both are aggregated onto the 100 m grid, so their fine-scale claim is weakened by resampling.
